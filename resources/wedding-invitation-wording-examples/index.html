The wording of a wedding invitation follows who is hosting and how formal the day is. Start with the hosting line, then add the date, the place and the reception.

The parts of a wedding invitation

  1. The hosting line: who invites the guest
  2. The request line: the words that ask for guests' company
  3. The names of the couple
  4. The date and the time
  5. The place, with the city
  6. The reception line
  7. The dress code and the reply details

Formal wording

Mr. and Mrs. Taylor request the honor of your presence at the marriage of their daughter Alex to Jamie Chen on Saturday, the sixth of June, at four o'clock in the afternoon. Reception to follow.
Together with their families, Alex Taylor and Jamie Chen request the pleasure of your company at their wedding. Saturday, the sixth of June, at four o'clock in the afternoon, at Pine Hall, Denver, Colorado. Dinner and dancing to follow.

Modern wording

Together with their families, Alex and Jamie invite you to celebrate their wedding. Saturday, June 6, at 4:00 in the afternoon. Reception to follow.
Alex and Jamie are getting married. Please join us for the ceremony and a dinner afterward. Kindly reply by May 1.

Casual wording

We're getting married. Come celebrate with us on June 6 at 4:00 pm. Food, music and dancing to follow.
Two people, one big party. Join us as Alex and Jamie say I do. June 6, 4:00 pm, Pine Hall.

The names, dates and addresses in these examples are placeholders. Replace each one with your own details.

Dress code, RSVP and the details card

  • Write the dress code under the venue line: black tie, formal, cocktail, semi-formal or casual.
  • Put the reply date and the way to reply on the response card. See wedding RSVP wording.
  • Use a details card or a wedding website for hotels, parking, the schedule and directions.

Common questions

Who should be named first on a wedding invitation?

The hosts come first. Hosts are the people paying or inviting, which can be the parents, the couple or both families.

How do I write the date and time formally?

Spell it out in words, as in Saturday, the sixth of June, at four o'clock in the afternoon. Modern invitations often use numerals.

Where does the dress code go?

Put it near the bottom of the invitation, under the place and reception lines, or on the details card.
