Cookie Exchange Invitation Wording

Cookie exchange invitation wording to copy, plus the rules to print on the card: how many cookies to bring, containers, recipes and allergy labels.

A cookie exchange invitation has to explain the deal. Guests need to know how many cookies to bake, what to bring them in, and what they take home.

Put the rules on the invitation

  1. How many dozen each guest brings
  2. What to bring them in: a container, a tin or a box, so they can carry the swap home
  3. A request to label the cookie type and any common allergens, such as nuts, eggs, dairy or wheat
  4. Whether to bring a copy of the recipe
  5. The reply date, so you can tell guests the final head count

Wording examples

You're invited to our annual cookie exchange. Bring two dozen of your favorite cookie and a container to take treats home. Saturday, December 12, 2:00 to 4:00 pm. Reply by December 5.
Let's swap. Bake two dozen cookies, label them, and leave with a mix of everyone's best. Please list any nuts or other allergens on your card. Sunday, December 13, at 3:00 pm.
Cookie swap. Bring a dozen cookies for every person who is coming, plus the recipe on a card. We will supply the cocoa and the tins.

The names, dates and addresses in these examples are placeholders. Replace each one with your own details.

Exchange or swap

Both words are in common use. Pick the one your friends say. The invitation works the same either way.

Common questions

How many cookies should each guest bring?

That is your choice as host. Two dozen is a common figure in invitation wording. Whatever number you pick, guests take home about the same amount they brought.

Should I ask for allergy labels?

Yes. A small card by each plate listing the cookie name and common allergens helps guests who need to avoid them.
